## Mathematical findings from code and independent checks

The opt-in `JointGraph` encodes `(1,1)` with coefficient `A_ij=1`, `(1,2)` with two coefficients `1/2`, and `(2,1)` with two coefficients `1`. Its energy is the sum of those weighted `delta[i,j]` terms plus `omega_warp` for either warp. Each matched patient state therefore contributes exactly one unit of correspondence weight. For every complete monotone path, `sum_j A_ij + U_i = 1`; averaging over paths preserves it. Consensus **occupancy** is a different variable: `O_j` is the probability that a match edge consumes state `j`, whereas `sum_i A_ij` can equal 2 for a certain `(2,1)` edge or 1/2 for a certain `(1,2)` edge. The valid replacement is `O_j + D_j = 1`, with `D_j` the sum of internal and terminal unmatched probabilities. The analogous expected-state counts are `E[M_patient]+E[U_patient]=n` and `E[M_consensus]+E[D_consensus]=t`; match-edge counts alone cannot replace either count.

I checked this without the repository oracle. On a `1×2` lattice, the four complete path energies are `delta00+eta_d+lambda_terminal_gap`, `delta01+eta_d+lambda_terminal_gap`, `(delta00+delta01)/2+omega_warp`, and `eta_i+2 eta_d+2 lambda_terminal_gap`. On a `2×1` lattice they are `delta00+eta_i+lambda_terminal_gap`, `delta10+eta_i+lambda_terminal_gap`, `delta00+delta10+omega_warp`, and `2 eta_i+eta_d+2 lambda_terminal_gap`. Hand-computed `logZ`, `A`, and `O` agreed with `infer_joint` within `1.2e-16` for nontrivial costs. The checked `(1,2)` path gives two half-weight correspondence columns but full occupancy of both; the `(2,1)` path gives correspondence column sum 2 but occupancy 1. The existing synthetic tests cover both warp directions and internal/terminal missing segments; these pass, but their cost matrices deliberately make one explanation dominant and do not test ambiguous alternatives.

For fixed edge posteriors, the expected expression objective is `Q(B)=sum_{p,i,j} A[p,i,j] delta[p,i,j](B)`. Since `delta=(1/G) sum_g precision[p,i,g] (x[p,i,g]-B[j,g])^2`, setting `dQ/dB[j,g]=0` gives the existing precision-weighted mean with weights `A`. Thus `_barycenter.sufficient_statistics` and `expected_match_energy` need no algebraic change **provided** `A` is the edge-energy coefficient specified above. An independent SciPy BFGS minimization on a random two-patient joint posterior agreed with the current M-step to `1.9e-8` in coordinates and `4.5e-16` in objective. This is stronger than the included test that wraps the same `log_a` in two posterior classes and compares identical downstream calls.

Old-mode compatibility is structural for this commit: AST comparison against `2571455` found `LatticeModel._build_graph`, `infer`, both forward/backward kernels, `fit`, all three barycenter functions, `project`, and `summarize` unchanged. The `graph` property only adds an early opt-in branch; old default arguments still choose `warp="off"`. Existing old lattice/API tests also pass. Focused run: `PYTHONPATH=src /data1/chanj3/wangm10/conda_envs/scdebussy-py311/bin/python -m pytest tests/test_v2_joint_warp.py tests/test_v2_lattice.py tests/test_v2_api.py -q` gave **288 passed**.

`lambda_terminal_gap` is currently a **per-side, per-terminal-run open charge**, added to ordinary per-state `eta_i`/`eta_d` extension cost. A nonempty leading and trailing terminal run can each pay it; the all-terminal path pays it twice, once per axis. Internal gaps instead pay `lambda_i`/`lambda_d` opens plus `eta` extension. The code and analytic `1×2`/`2×1` checks support this interpretation. Merely requiring `lambda_terminal_gap > 0` is not a calibrated protection against all-terminal collapse: with a `1×1` cost of 10, `eta_i=eta_d=1`, `lambda_terminal_gap=.01`, `beta=1`, the all-terminal posterior is 0.999658. That can be legitimate for an unmatchable trajectory, but occupancy and no-match diagnostics must expose it.

## Implementation concerns, ranked

1. **High — oracle independence is incomplete.** `enumerate_joint_paths` independently sums complete path probabilities instead of using forward/backward, so it can catch inference and marginal-aggregation errors. It reads the *same compiled graph, energies, and annotations* as `infer_joint`, so a wrong edge topology, cost, or annotation can make both agree. Add hand-enumerated or separately constructed path tables for at least one mixed `2×3` and one masked/terminal case before trusting the graph compiler. The `1×2`/`2×1` analytic checks above cover only minimal boundaries.
2. **High — joint rejection is not yet defined.** `JointPosterior` has expected transition counts and occupancies but no distribution of matched patient/consensus state counts; old `low_match_probability` and `no_match_probability` depend on a different count distribution. A later fit must either compute the joint matched-state count distribution or explicitly choose and validate a new posterior support rule. Reusing old `count_probability`, MAP gap counts, or a thresholded mean occupancy without explanation would make reported rejection/coverage incomparable.
3. **Medium — terminal-cost calibration.** Positive terminal open cost alone does not prevent all-terminal paths, and a fixed open cost makes a long terminal tail comparatively cheap after its first state. Inspect posterior all-terminal probability and per-patient terminal mass over the intended HTAN grid. Record both open and extension costs; do not call `lambda_terminal_gap` a per-state penalty.
4. **Medium — the current barycenter test is tautological.** The mathematical update and independent numerical minimization pass, but Phase 3 must verify the *E-step builds costs from the current barycenter* using exactly the correspondence coefficients consumed by the M-step. Test EM descent and final returned-posterior consistency over several E/M iterations, including a forced `(2,1)` case.
5. **Medium — graph and diagnostic scale.** The joint graph is rebuilt per patient on each E-step; check runtime and peak memory at 100×100 landmarks and verify no overflow/underflow or negative occupancy artifacts. A valid DAG on tiny fixtures alone does not establish robust HTAN execution.

No definite Phase 1–2 mathematical defect was found in the audited cases. These concerns are verification and interpretation gaps, not evidence that the unfinished model fails.

## Prespecified HTAN design audit

A maximum of six candidates with the frozen trajectory-250/unit-precision panel is a reasonable **screening** grid if all `omega_warp` and terminal settings are written to versioned configs before results are inspected. Compare against raw `s_local`, original v2, the restricted 250/unit fit, and the prior fixed-consensus DTW diagnostic. The old eight-patient controls were raw coverage/purity `0.865/0.772`, restricted 250/unit `0.863/0.769`, and moderate DTW `0.870/0.763`; RU1303 raw overlap with other patients' SCLC-N was `0.531` and the prototype reached `0.100` while losing purity. Keep metrics on exactly the same input cells, patient order, bins, colors, and `[0,1]` axis. The prototype's inherited support flag is not a calibrated comparator for joint rejection.

This grid can show whether the *observed RU1303 pathology* improves, but it cannot alone prove general biological alignment: the panel and cost hypothesis were developed on this same cohort, six candidate outcomes still invite selection, and global purity is dominated by larger patients. Report each patient's coverage, purity, rejection, displacement, held-out-marker agreement, and lineage positions; use patient-level summaries or a patient-block uncertainty analysis rather than cell-level pseudo-replication. Freeze which candidate is evaluated on any independent cohort or leave-one-patient-out confirmation after this screen.

Required controls/diagnostics in addition to the stated grid:

- One **joint-graph no-warp control** with warp cost large enough to suppress both warp edges and the same terminal/internal penalties as its paired candidate. This isolates improvement due to warp from improvement due merely to changed terminal-gap pricing. It is a diagnostic control, not another winner eligible for selection.
- Verify raw-input scoring and old restricted fit reproduce their cached values on the exact eight patients; check cell IDs/order and input SHA256. Confirm held-out markers are absent from fit genes, and the 250-gene list and selection settings exactly match the frozen files.
- Per-patient posterior decomposition: `(1,1)/(1,2)/(2,1)` mass, internal and terminal patient/consensus unmatched mass, all-terminal path probability, matched-state support, and rejected/finite-unsupported fractions. Show RU1303's terminal consensus mass and cost-surface direction; distinguish a plausible reparameterization from deletion of difficult cells or a late endpoint pile-up.
- Plot every patient map, with reversal, plateau, compression, jump, and endpoint flags; quantify displacement and coverage for already well-aligned patients, especially RU1083, RU1444, and RU581. Show per-patient held-out marker trajectories and aggregate concordance, including markers that worsen.
- Report results of the full fixed grid and the selection rule before a heatmap-led choice. For any selected candidate, show sensitivity to leaving RU1303 out of aggregate metrics so that one case does not mask deterioration elsewhere.

## Decision categories for the later experiment

- **PASS (scientific candidate):** synthetic cases separate warp, internal missingness and terminal missingness; posterior identities/EM/projection checks pass; at least one prespecified joint fit improves purity over the paired restricted 250/unit fit and raw `s_local` (`0.769` and `0.772`), holds mean coverage near raw (`>=0.855` as a prespecified 0.01 tolerance), materially lowers RU1303 overlap from `0.531` without low support or endpoint collapse, and does not materially harm other patients or aggregate held-out marker concordance. Report patient-block uncertainty and seek an independent cohort before default promotion.
- **FAIL:** a graph/EM/projection invariant fails; the apparent RU1303 fix is driven by rejection, internal/terminal deletion, or compression unsupported by the cost surface; purity or coverage drops substantially; or formerly aligned patients deteriorate while the global average hides it.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** invariants hold but metrics trade off, improvements are within patient-level uncertainty, marker effects conflict, or the same-cohort screen cannot distinguish warp benefit from terminal pricing. A visually cleaner heatmap by itself is inconclusive.

The `0.855` coverage floor and raw/restricted purity comparisons are proposed audit criteria for the upcoming screen; they were specified here without inspecting any Phase 3–4 HTAN outcomes. They are not a claim that a result already passes.
